# Sources and evidence strength

Use sources to sharpen a testable claim, not to decorate an answer. Record the source, claim, context, and strength.

| Evidence class | Typical strength | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Repeated representative performance | Strong for the stated task and conditions | Decide whether the target behavior is changing |
| Delayed retrieval or transfer | Strong for durability and flexibility | Separate immediate fluency from learning |
| Controlled or comparable intervention result | Context-dependent to strong | Compare a change when conditions are similar |
| Process trace or error pattern | Useful diagnostic evidence | Generate competing explanations |
| Self-report or confidence | Contextual | Explain conditions; never substitute for performance |
| Historical or philosophical principle | Practice inspiration | Translate into behavior and test; do not treat as proof |
| Prior AI output | Hypothesis only | Audit against current evidence |

For modern research or historical claims, use an identifiable public source and avoid invented quotations. Distinguish “supported more strongly,” “context-dependent,” and “practice inspiration.” If the source does not justify the claim under the user's conditions, write `UNKNOWN` rather than expanding the claim.
