# Evidence and decision loop

## Evidence ledger

| Type | What to record | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FACT | Direct observation and conditions | `10/20` correct in 30 minutes, closed book |
| JUDGMENT | Interpretation with confidence | `Transfer may be unstable; confidence medium` |
| ACTION | One concrete intervention | `Complete six unfamiliar variants without notes` |
| VALIDATION | Test that can change the judgment | `Repeat in 48 hours with a changed surface` |
| UNKNOWN | Missing fact that matters | `Time pressure versus retrieval is not separated` |

For every important claim, keep provenance and falsifiers:

```text
Claim:
Evidence:
Conditions:
Confidence: low | medium | high
What would weaken or falsify it:
Next evidence:
```

Prefer repeated representative performance, delayed retrieval and transfer, error patterns, process traces, comparable intervention results, self-report, then generic advice or authority claims. Self-report provides context; it does not substitute for performance evidence.

## Bottleneck diagnosis

Compare the target behavior with the observed gap. Ask which layer first prevents success:

- exposure: the material or procedure has not been encountered;
- understanding: the principle cannot be explained or connected;
- retrieval: it cannot be produced without cues;
- procedure: steps break down under time or load;
- transfer: a changed surface defeats the same underlying idea;
- retention: performance decays after delay;
- environment: tools, schedule, fatigue, or stakes change the task;
- energy or motivation: the person knows how but cannot reliably begin or sustain it.

Keep competing explanations visible. A single score or session is not a stable trait.

## Decision update

Use explicit rules:

```text
If the intervention improves the target on immediate and delayed/transfer tests,
  keep it and gradually vary the task.
If immediate performance improves but delayed/transfer performance does not,
  reduce recognition and add cue-free, varied retrieval.
If no measure moves after a bounded trial,
  revisit the bottleneck or change one major variable.
If risk, uncertainty, or stakes exceed the evidence,
  stop ordinary optimization and escalate to the appropriate source.
```

Set a re-test date at the time of action. A plan without a measurement and update rule is only a suggestion.
