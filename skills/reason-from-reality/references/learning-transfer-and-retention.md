# Learning, transfer, and retention

## Keep performance layers separate

```text
Exposure → explanation → cue-free retrieval → procedure
→ transfer to a changed surface → delayed retention
```

- **Exposure:** the learner has seen or attempted the material.
- **Explanation:** the learner can state the principle and conditions.
- **Retrieval:** the learner can produce it without the source in view.
- **Procedure:** the learner can execute the steps accurately under the target constraints.
- **Transfer:** the learner can recognize and apply the idea when wording, context, or surface form changes.
- **Retention:** the performance remains after a meaningful delay.

A fluent explanation or immediate rework can be useful evidence, but neither alone proves durable learning.

## A small study experiment

1. Pick one target behavior and representative task set.
2. Record an independent baseline under the real time and tool conditions.
3. Apply one main intervention, such as cue-free retrieval or varied transfer practice.
4. Measure immediate accuracy, error type, time, and confidence separately.
5. Re-test on a changed task at a delayed interval.
6. Update the next practice block from the result.

Do not add every method at once when attribution matters. Let the schedule serve the bottleneck rather than the other way around.

## Role of AI

AI can explain, generate variants, give feedback, check a rubric, and schedule tests. The learner should still perform the target retrieval, derivation, writing, problem solving, or programming. When AI supplies a solution, record that as assisted performance, not independent mastery.
